# Top lane cheat sheet

- Slug: top
- Role: Top
- Summary: Win or survive a long solo lane, then turn it into side-lane pressure or a strong teamfight job.
- Where you play: The top lane and the top half of the map, near Void Grubs, Rift Herald and Baron.
- You win by: Lane control, side-lane pressure and choosing the right teamfight job.
- Starter champions: garen, malphite, darius, sett, ornn, shen
- Key lessons: MI01, MI06, MI15, MA40, MA11, MA06, MA15, MA26, MI13

## Short answer

Top is the most isolated lane. You play a long one-versus-one against a durable opponent, usually a fighter or tank, far from your team and far from dragon. That means two things. First, the laning phase is about your own decisions: trading around cooldowns, managing the wave and respecting the enemy jungler. Second, your value later comes from pressure your team cannot provide: holding a side lane, pulling enemies towards you, flanking or standing at the front of a fight. Top laners who climb do not just win lane; they decide what that lead or deficit means for the whole map.

## Your jobs

1. **Farm safely through a long lane.** Top waves take a long walk from your turret, so a bad wave position leaves you exposed for a long time.
2. **Manage the wave on purpose.** Choose between freezing, slow pushing and crashing based on the matchup and where the enemy jungler is.
3. **Track the enemy jungler.** A top laner pushed past the middle of the lane with no vision is the easiest gank on the map.
4. **Support top-side objectives.** Void Grubs, Rift Herald and later Baron are on your side. Your wave state decides whether you can help.
5. **Pick your teamfight job.** Front line, flank or side lane. Decide before the fight starts, not during it.

## Laning phase

- **Know the first three levels.** Before the game, learn who wins level 1, 2 and 3 in your matchup. Level 2 comes after the first full wave plus three melee minions of the second wave. The first player to level 2 can often trade for free.
- **Trade when their key ability is down.** Most top lane trades are decided by one ability: a dash, a shield or a heal. Wait for it to be used, then step forward.
- **Respect minion damage.** Fighting inside a large enemy wave usually loses even if your champion is stronger. Thin the wave or fight on your side of it.
- **Freeze when you are ahead or in danger.** Holding the wave just outside your turret forces your opponent to walk into gank range to farm, and keeps you safe.
- **Crash before you recall.** Push the wave into the enemy turret, then recall, so you lose little farm and experience while you shop.
- **Ward at the right time.** Place a ward in the river or the side bush shortly before the enemy jungler could reach top, not at the start of lane.
- **Use Teleport well if you take it.** Teleport lets you return to lane quickly after a recall or join a fight elsewhere. Track the enemy top laner's Teleport too; a top laner without it is much less dangerous to the rest of the map.

## Mid game

- **Hold a side lane with a plan.** Split push when you know where the enemy team is and your team can avoid a fight without you. Leave before several enemies can reach you.
- **Draw enemies to you.** If two enemies come to stop you, your team should have a numbers advantage elsewhere. Ping that you are drawing attention.
- **Help top-side objectives.** Push your wave before Void Grubs, Rift Herald or Baron so you arrive first and do not lose a wave by joining.
- **Trade across the map.** When the enemy team takes a dragon far from you, taking a turret or plates top can be the better trade than walking across the map late.
- **Know your spikes.** Many top champions get much stronger at a certain level or item. Fight at your spike; avoid fights at theirs.

## Teamfights

- **Tanks start and absorb.** Engage on the most valuable target you can reach, or stand in front of your carries and punish whoever walks in.
- **Fighters flank.** Approach from the side once the enemy has used key crowd control, then attack the closest valuable target.
- **Do not go in alone.** Engage only when your team is close enough to follow within a few seconds.
- **Splitting is a teamfight job too.** If your team can avoid fighting, holding a side lane can be worth more than joining a five-versus-five.

## What to track

- Where the enemy jungler was last seen and which side they are likely on.
- The enemy top laner's Flash and Teleport timers.
- Your wave state every time you leave lane: pushing, frozen or about to crash.
- Who is stronger right now: you, your opponent or neither.

## Common mistakes

- **Mistake:** Pushing to the enemy turret every wave with no vision. **Fix:** Push only when you know the enemy jungler is elsewhere or you plan to recall.
- **Mistake:** Trading while the enemy has their key ability ready. **Fix:** Wait for it to be used, then trade before it comes back.
- **Mistake:** Teleporting into a fight that is already lost. **Fix:** Teleport only when the fight is still even and you can land somewhere safe.
- **Mistake:** Split pushing when your team is about to fight. **Fix:** Ping your plan and group if the team cannot avoid the fight.

## Going deeper

**Play the matchup, not the champion.** High-ranked top laners know the few abilities that decide each matchup and plan their trades around them. Keep short notes for your most common matchups.

**Lose lane gracefully.** In a hard matchup, your goal is to stay close in farm and experience, not to win. Freeze near your turret, give up some minions safely and wait for your scaling or your jungler.

**Your wave is your timer.** Before you rotate, ask how long until your wave reaches your turret. Leaving a bouncing wave at the right moment costs you little; leaving a large wave pushing into you can cost a turret.

## Practice plan

1. In your next three games, note who would win level 2 in your matchup and test it on the first wave.
2. Before each trade, name the enemy ability you are waiting for.
3. After each game, find one moment where you left lane and check your wave state at that time.
