# Objective setup

- Stable lesson ID: MA41
- Slug: objective-setup
- Category: Macro
- Roles and level: All roles; intermediate to advanced.
- Summary: Win objectives before they spawn by resetting, pushing waves and taking vision in order.
- Prerequisites: Objective control, Timers, Lane priority.
- Observable action: Before each objective, say which step your team is on: reset, push, vision, group or start.

## Short answer

Most objective fights are decided before the objective spawns. The team that has reset, pushed its nearby waves and taken vision arrives first, with full health and items, and controls the area. The team that notices the timer late arrives one by one through unwarded ground. Set up early, in order, and the fight becomes easier or unnecessary.

## How it works

Think of the setup as a sequence that ends when the objective spawns. If you start late, cut steps from the front of the list rather than skipping vision.

1. **Reset and buy.** Recall early enough that everyone is full health with their items when the window opens.
2. **Push nearby waves.** Push the lanes closest to the objective. Enemies then have to choose between losing minions and joining the fight late.
3. **Take vision.** Sweep and ward the entrances on the objective's side of the river before the enemy arrives.
4. **Group and hold.** Move together and claim the space. Arriving as a group is safer than arriving one at a time.
5. **Start or trade.** If you control the area, start the objective. If you do not, take something elsewhere instead of forcing a bad fight.

## Hypothetical example

A dragon will spawn soon. Your bot lane has a big wave about to crash into the enemy turret, and your mid laner is low on health. The mid laner recalls now. Bot lane crashes the wave, then walks into river with the support placing wards. The jungler sweeps the nearest enemy ward. When the dragon spawns, your team is already there with full health. The enemy bot lane is stuck catching the crashed wave. The enemy now has to fight four against five, or give up the dragon.

## Going deeper

**Lane priority is the foundation.** The lanes closest to the objective need to be able to move first. For dragon that usually means mid and bot; for objectives on the top side it usually means mid and top. If those lanes are losing their waves, the setup starts by fixing that, not by walking to the objective.

**Trading objectives is a real option.** If the enemy has set up better, contesting is often a mistake. Take a different objective, a turret or the enemy's jungle on the other side of the map. A clean trade can be worth more than a risky fight.

**Watch for the enemy's setup too.** If enemy laners recall at the same time or their waves push into your turrets, they are probably preparing for the objective. Match their timing or plan a trade.

**Smite and the start.** Starting an objective while the enemy is close invites a steal or a fight in a bad spot. Clear vision and push the enemy back first, then start. Keep track of the enemy jungler's Smite and position if you can.

**Later objectives raise the stakes.** Baron and late dragons can decide the game. The same steps apply, but mistakes cost more. Respect long death timers: losing a fight at a late objective can end the game.

## Common mistake and correction

**Mistake:** Walking to the objective as it spawns, leaving waves unpushed and entering unwarded river alone.

**Correction:** Start the sequence earlier. If you are late, choose a safer option elsewhere instead of fighting at a disadvantage.

## Practice

For your next three games, pause the replay shortly before each major objective spawn. Name which of the five steps each teammate had completed. Write down the first step your team missed and how the objective went. Look for a pattern across games.

## Limits

Spawn times, objective rewards and map details change between seasons. Check the current in-game timer and patch notes. The sequence is a planning aid, not a guaranteed result.
