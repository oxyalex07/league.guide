# Mental game

- Stable lesson ID: MA48
- Slug: mental-game
- Category: Macro
- Roles and level: All roles; beginner to advanced.
- Summary: Protect your decision-making from tilt with clear limits and a focus on what you control.
- Prerequisites: Deliberate practice, Communication.
- Observable action: Set a stopping rule before your session and follow it.

## Short answer

Frustration changes how you play. After a bad death or a losing streak, players often take riskier fights, stop watching the map and argue with teammates. This is often called tilt. The best players are not immune to it; they notice it and protect their decisions. Set limits before you play, focus on what you control and step away when your play changes.

## How it works

Plan your session before frustration arrives.

1. **Set a stopping rule.** For example, stop after a set number of losses in a row or after a set time. Decide before you start.
2. **Focus on what you control.** Your decisions, your focus for the game and your communication. Teammates' choices are outside your control.
3. **Notice the signs.** Typing more, taking fights you would normally avoid or feeling rushed are signs your play is changing.
4. **Reset.** Take a short break, stretch or step away from the screen before the next game.

## Hypothetical example

You lose two close games in a row and feel annoyed. Your stopping rule is two losses in a row, so you take a break instead of queueing again. You review one moment from each game and play again later with a clear focus. The next session starts with your normal decision-making instead of frustration.

## Going deeper

**Mute early.** If chat distracts you, mute it at the start of the game. You can still use pings and timers. This removes a major source of frustration.

**Separate results from performance.** You can play well and lose, or play poorly and win. Judge your game by your focus and decisions.

**One game at a time.** A losing streak does not change what the next game asks of you. Avoid queueing to win back lost points; queue when you are ready to play well.

**Physical basics matter.** Sleep, food and breaks affect focus. Playing tired often leads to worse decisions.

**Positive actions help the team.** A calm ping or a short plan helps teammates focus. Arguments rarely change the outcome and often make it worse.

## Common mistake and correction

**Mistake:** Queueing again immediately after a frustrating loss to win back points.

**Correction:** Follow your stopping rule, take a short break and return with a clear focus.

## Practice

Write a stopping rule before your next three sessions. After each session, note whether you followed it and how your last game felt.

## Limits

This lesson offers general habits for focus during games. It is not medical or mental-health advice. If gaming stress affects your wellbeing, consider talking with someone you trust or a professional.
