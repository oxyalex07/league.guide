# Champion pool

- Stable lesson ID: MA43
- Slug: champion-pool
- Category: Macro
- Roles and level: All roles; intermediate to advanced.
- Summary: Play a small pool deeply so your attention goes to the game, not your own champion.
- Prerequisites: Matchup preparation, Power spikes.
- Observable action: Choose the few champions you will play this week and stick to them.

## Short answer

Most climbing players play a small number of champions. When you know a champion deeply, your hands handle the combos and your eyes can watch the map, the waves and the enemy cooldowns. When you switch champions every game, your attention goes to remembering what your abilities do. A small pool is not about one-tricking forever; it is about spending your attention where it wins games.

## How it works

Build a pool that covers your role without spreading you thin.

1. **Choose a main.** Pick one champion you enjoy and that fits your role. This is the champion you will play most often.
2. **Add one or two backups.** Choose champions that cover your main's problems: one for when your main is banned and one for matchups where your main struggles.
3. **Keep a secondary role ready.** In solo queue you will sometimes not get your first role. Have one or two simple, reliable champions for your second role.
4. **Stay with the pool.** Avoid adding new champions in ranked unless you have practised them elsewhere.

## Hypothetical example

You are a mid laner who enjoys a control mage. Your pool is that mage, a second mage with more mobility for matchups against assassins, and a safe pick that rarely loses lane badly for blind picks. For your secondary role, support, you keep one engage champion. Over a few weeks your understanding of each grows, and your decisions in lane get faster because you no longer think about your own buttons.

## Going deeper

**Depth reveals things you cannot learn from switching.** After many games on one champion, you learn exact trading patterns, how your power changes with each item and which matchups need a different plan. You start to see options that a casual player of that champion never notices.

**Blind picks and counter picks.** If you pick early in draft, choose a champion that is hard to counter. If you pick late, you can choose a champion that counters the enemy's. Keeping one safe blind pick and one counter pick in your pool covers both cases.

**Match the pool to how you like to win.** Some players enjoy controlling waves and scaling; others prefer early aggression or roaming. A pool built around your style keeps you consistent.

**Expanding without losing ranks.** If you want a new champion, play it in normal games or practice first. Bring it to ranked once you know its trading pattern and power spikes.

**Patches move pools.** A balance change can weaken your main. Usually your knowledge outweighs small changes, but if your main becomes clearly weak, rely more on your backups.

## Common mistake and correction

**Mistake:** Picking a new champion every few games because it looked strong in a video or on a tier list.

**Correction:** Keep a small pool, and practise new champions outside ranked before adding them.

## Practice

Write your pool down: a main, one or two backups and a secondary-role pick. Play only those champions in ranked for the next two weeks, then review which one gave you the most consistent decisions.

## Limits

Champion strength changes with patches. Pool advice is a general approach; some players climb by playing one champion only, and others play several well.
