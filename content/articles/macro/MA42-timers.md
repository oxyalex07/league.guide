# Timers

- Stable lesson ID: MA42
- Slug: timers
- Category: Macro
- Roles and level: All roles; intermediate to advanced.
- Summary: Track when key resources return so you can act before the enemy does.
- Prerequisites: Cooldown tracking, Jungle tracking.
- Observable action: After an enemy uses a summoner spell or an objective is taken, write or ping when it comes back.

## Short answer

Many of the biggest decisions in League depend on time: when an enemy's Flash returns, when a dragon or Baron spawns again, when a jungle camp respawns and when a dead opponent comes back. Players who track these timers arrive early and prepared. Players who do not arrive late and guess. You do not need to memorise every number. You need a habit of noting the important ones and saying them out loud to your team.

## How it works

A timer is only useful if it changes a decision. Start with the timers that most often decide fights and objectives, and add others as the habit becomes automatic.

1. **Summoner spells.** When an enemy uses Flash or another key spell, note when it returns. A laner without Flash is much easier to kill for a while.
2. **Objectives.** When a dragon, Baron or other major objective is taken, check the in-game timer for its next spawn and plan your setup around it.
3. **Death timers.** When an enemy dies, glance at the scoreboard. A long death timer is a window to take something.
4. **Jungle camps and buffs.** Note when the enemy jungler took a buff or camp. Its respawn tells you where they will want to be later.

## Hypothetical example

At the start of a fight, the enemy mid laner uses Flash to escape. You ping it and type when it comes back. A few minutes later, your jungler asks whether to gank mid or bot. Mid still has no Flash, so the gank there is far more likely to work. One small note made that decision easy.

## Going deeper

**Write the expiry time, not the duration.** "Mid flash 14:20" is easier to use than "mid flash used". Everyone can compare it with the game clock. Many teams use the scoreboard or chat to share these quickly; use whatever your team reads.

**Objective timers drive the whole map.** Work backwards from the next spawn. Before an objective, your team should be reset, nearby waves pushed and vision placed. The objective setup lesson walks through the order. If you notice the timer late, you will usually arrive without that preparation.

**Death timers grow during the game.** Early deaths are short, so a kill rarely leads straight to a big objective. Later, a single death can last long enough to take a Baron or an inhibitor. Check the scoreboard after every late-game kill before deciding what to do.

**Buff and camp timers reveal pathing.** If you saw the enemy jungler take a buff, you know roughly when it respawns and that they are likely to return for it. That helps you predict their position, ward their path or take the camp yourself.

**Item timers matter too.** When an enemy finishes a key item or their first big purchase, their strength jumps. Watching their gold and recall timing helps you avoid fighting just after their spike.

## Common mistake and correction

**Mistake:** Noticing an enemy is missing Flash, then forgetting it until it is already back.

**Correction:** Turn each important event into a written or pinged time the moment it happens. If the note is in chat, your whole team can use it.

## Practice

For three games, track only Flash for the enemy laner you face and the next dragon spawn. After each game, check how many times the note changed what you did. Add one more timer type once those two feel automatic.

## Limits

Cooldowns and spawn times change between patches and can be affected by items, runes and game modes. Use the in-game timers and tooltips for exact values. This lesson teaches the habit, not current numbers.
