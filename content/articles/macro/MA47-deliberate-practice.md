# Deliberate practice

- Stable lesson ID: MA47
- Slug: deliberate-practice
- Category: Macro
- Roles and level: All roles; beginner to advanced.
- Summary: Improve faster with one focus per game and a short replay review afterwards.
- Prerequisites: Win conditions, Champion pool.
- Observable action: Before queueing, write one thing you will practise this game.

## Short answer

Playing many games is not the same as improving. Players who climb steadily choose one skill to work on, play with that focus and check afterwards whether they did it. A short, honest replay review teaches more than another game played on autopilot. This lesson gives you a simple loop: choose, play, review, adjust.

## How it works

Use a loop that fits into normal play.

1. **Choose one focus.** Pick a single, observable habit, such as checking the minimap after every last hit or tracking the enemy jungler's first clear.
2. **Play with the focus.** Remind yourself during the game. Your result matters less than whether you practised the habit.
3. **Review quickly.** After the game, watch a few key moments: your deaths, an objective fight and one lane phase.
4. **Adjust.** Keep the focus if you still struggle with it, or choose a new one once it feels automatic.

## Hypothetical example

You notice you often die to ganks. Your focus becomes: ping and check where the enemy jungler was last seen before every trade. In the next game you still die once, but in the review you see you forgot to check before that trade. You keep the same focus for another two games until it becomes automatic.

## Going deeper

**Review deaths first.** For each death, ask what information you had, which enemy cooldowns were up and where your teammates were. Most deaths come from missing information, poor position or ignoring a cooldown.

**Judge decisions, not results.** A good decision can lose a fight, and a bad one can work by luck. Pause before the decision and use only the information you had then.

**Compare your numbers with yourself.** Track simple numbers like creep score at a fixed time or deaths before a certain minute. Compare them with your own recent games, not with a fixed target.

**Watch strong players with a question.** Watching high-ranked players helps most when you look for one thing, such as how they position before an objective or when they recall.

**Keep sessions focused.** Long sessions can make attention drop. Short breaks between games help you stay sharp and review honestly.

## Common mistake and correction

**Mistake:** Queueing again straight after each game without learning anything specific.

**Correction:** Take a few minutes between games to review one moment and choose or keep a focus.

## Practice

Keep a short note for your next five games: the focus, whether you did it, and one moment from the review. After five games, choose the next focus based on the notes.

## Limits

Improvement takes time and depends on many factors. This loop is a structure for practice, not a guaranteed result.
