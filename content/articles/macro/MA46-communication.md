# Communication

- Stable lesson ID: MA46
- Slug: communication
- Category: Macro
- Roles and level: All roles; beginner to advanced.
- Summary: Share timers, threats and plans with short, calm pings.
- Prerequisites: Timers, Map awareness.
- Observable action: Ping one useful piece of information each minute: a missing enemy, a timer or your next plan.

## Short answer

In solo queue, your team does not share your screen. A short, calm ping turns your knowledge into team knowledge: where the enemy jungler was seen, that the enemy mid has no Flash, that you are coming to help. Good communication is not about leading or criticising. It is about giving teammates information early enough to use.

## How it works

Focus on information that changes someone else's decision.

1. **Missing enemies.** Ping when your opponent leaves lane. Lanes close to their likely destination need to know.
2. **Timers.** Share summoner spell and objective timers so teammates can plan.
3. **Your plan.** Use "on my way" or a short message to say where you will be, so teammates can act with you.
4. **Danger.** Warn a teammate who is pushing into an unseen area.

## Hypothetical example

You see the enemy jungler take a top-side camp. You ping their position and type that bot lane is safe for now. Your bot lane trades aggressively, wins a fight and pushes the wave. A minute later, you ping that the jungler is missing again, and bot lane backs off. Two pings shaped your bot lane's decisions.

## Going deeper

**Be early and specific.** Information is most useful before teammates need it. "Jungler top side" is more useful than "careful" after a gank has started.

**Plans beat demands.** "I will push mid and come to dragon" invites teammates to join. Spamming pings at a teammate rarely changes their mind and often makes them play worse.

**Keep tone calm.** Teammates who feel blamed often stop listening. Mute chat if it distracts you; you can still read pings and timers. Your focus is a resource.

**Learn the ping wheel.** Each ping type sends a different meaning. Use the most specific ping available, such as enemy missing, vision or on my way, rather than generic alerts.

**Listen as well as speak.** Read your teammates' pings and timers. When several players share information, the whole team can make better decisions.

## Common mistake and correction

**Mistake:** Using pings to criticise teammates, or pinging too late for the information to matter.

**Correction:** Ping information and plans early, and keep the tone neutral. Mute rather than argue.

## Practice

For three games, track how many useful pings you send: missing enemies, timers and plans. Aim for one meaningful ping each minute in the early game. After each game, note one ping that changed a teammate's decision.

## Limits

Communication helps only when teammates read it. Some players will ignore pings; focus on what you control.
