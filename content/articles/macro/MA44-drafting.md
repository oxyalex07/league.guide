# Drafting

- Stable lesson ID: MA44
- Slug: drafting
- Category: Macro
- Roles and level: All roles; intermediate to advanced.
- Summary: Read both team compositions in draft and play the win condition they create.
- Prerequisites: Win conditions, Champion pool.
- Observable action: In loading, describe both teams in one sentence each: how they want to win and what they lack.

## Short answer

Every team composition has a way it wants to win and things it lacks. Some teams want to fight in tight spaces; some want to poke from range; some want to catch one enemy alone; some want to stall until their carries are strong. Reading both drafts tells you what fights to take, which side of the map matters and when your team is strongest. You often cannot control the draft in solo queue, but you can always read it.

## How it works

Check both teams for a few basic ingredients and the general plan they suggest.

1. **Engage.** Who can start a fight? A team with no reliable engage needs the enemy to come to them.
2. **Frontline.** Who can absorb damage? Without a frontline, carries are exposed.
3. **Damage type.** Is the damage mixed between physical and magic? A team with only one type is easier to itemise against.
4. **Range and waveclear.** Can the team clear waves and defend turrets? Poke and siege teams rely on this.
5. **Scaling.** Which team is stronger early, and which grows stronger later?

## Hypothetical example

Your team has a tank with strong engage, two champions who fight well in groups and a scaling marksman. The enemy has long-range poke and little frontline. Your plan: avoid long sieges where they can poke you down, look for fights around objectives in tight jungle areas where your engage reaches them, and protect the marksman until they scale. Their plan is the opposite: poke at range, siege turrets and avoid being caught in close fights.

## Going deeper

**Common composition styles.** Teamfight or engage teams want five-versus-five fights in tight spaces. Poke and siege teams want to deal damage from range before fights start. Pick teams want to catch one enemy at a time with vision and crowd control. Protect teams build around one strong carry and keep them alive. Split-push teams create side-lane pressure so the enemy cannot group freely. Most real drafts mix these, but one plan usually leads.

**Your role in the composition.** Your champion's job depends on the team. A bruiser in a pick team might flank; the same bruiser in a protect team might stay near the carry. Read your job from the draft, not only from your champion.

**Draft order in solo queue.** Picking early means you may be countered, so safer champions help. Picking late lets you counter the opponent or fill what your team lacks, for example a frontline or engage. Use bans on champions that are strong against your pool or that you find hardest to play against.

**Scaling changes the plan over time.** If your team is stronger early, look to take objectives and pressure before the enemy reaches their items. If your team scales better, trade efficiently, avoid unnecessary early fights and give up objectives you cannot contest safely.

**Itemise against the draft.** The enemy composition affects your items: healing reduction against sustain, defensive items against burst and so on. Check current item options for your patch.

## Common mistake and correction

**Mistake:** Playing every game the same way regardless of the compositions, for example taking long poke fights with an engage team.

**Correction:** Name both teams' plans in loading. Choose fights and objectives that fit yours and deny theirs.

## Practice

In your next five games, write one sentence for each team's plan during loading. After the game, compare the plan with how the important fights actually started.

## Limits

Composition labels are simplifications; real games depend on execution, gold leads and individual matchups. Use them as a starting frame, not a fixed rule.
