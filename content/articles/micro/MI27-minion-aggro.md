# Minion aggro

- Stable lesson ID: MI27
- Slug: minion-aggro
- Category: Micro
- Roles and level: All laners and supports; beginner to advanced.
- Summary: Trade where enemy minions cannot punish you, and use their targeting on purpose.
- Prerequisites: Spacing, Trading.
- Observable action: Before damaging the enemy champion, check whether you are standing inside their minions' attack range.

## Short answer

Minions normally attack other minions. When you damage an enemy champion while standing near enemy minions, those minions switch to you for a short time. This is minion aggro. A trade that looks even between the two champions can become a clear loss once the minion damage is added. Strong laners decide where a trade happens before deciding whether to take it.

## How it works

Enemy minions answer a call for help. If you damage their champion, nearby enemy minions treat you as a priority target. Turrets follow the same idea: damaging an enemy champion inside their turret's range draws the turret onto you. The aggro does not last forever. Walk out of their range and the minions go back to other targets.

1. Look at the enemy wave before you trade. Count roughly how many minions are close enough to reach you.
2. Prefer trades when the enemy steps toward your wave, when their wave is small, or when your wave is clearly bigger.
3. After the trade, walk back toward your own minions so any aggro you drew falls off quickly.

## Hypothetical example

The enemy laner stands just behind six healthy minions. You could walk in and land a full combo, and the champion damage would favour you. Then six minions attack you while you walk out. Instead, wait until the enemy walks forward to last-hit a minion near your side. Now trade where their minions are busy with yours. The champion damage is the same and you take much less in return.

## Going deeper

**Wave size changes the price of every trade.** When your wave is bigger, enemy minions die faster and there are fewer of them to draw. That is one reason a slow-pushed wave creates trading pressure: the opponent has to farm near a large wave that hurts them if they fight back. When the enemy wave is bigger, short trades from range or no trades at all are often correct.

**Aggro can move a wave.** Drawing enemy minions onto you and then walking back pulls them toward your side. Some players use this to bring a wave closer to their turret or to set up a freeze. It costs health, so use it when the wave position is worth more than the damage taken.

**Turret aggro decides dives and lane fights.** Damaging an enemy champion under their turret draws turret shots even if you were attacking minions a moment earlier. Before a dive, decide who takes turret aggro and when they leave its range. In your own lane, fighting near your turret means an enemy who damages you gets punished by it.

**Ranged and melee champions feel this differently.** A ranged champion can often hit a champion standing at the edge of the wave and step back before taking much. A melee champion usually has to enter the wave to trade, so they need a bigger reward or an enemy who has walked out.

**Supports and bot lane.** In a two-versus-two lane, both players can draw aggro. If your partner trades inside the enemy wave while you trade from outside it, your team takes more damage than the numbers on the champions suggest. Agree on when you trade together.

## Common mistake and correction

**Mistake:** Judging a trade only by the champions' health bars and ignoring the minions that kept hitting you afterwards.

**Correction:** Count the minion damage as part of the trade. If you would be standing inside a large enemy wave, wait for a better spot or make the trade shorter.

## Practice

Play three games with one rule: before every trade, glance at the enemy wave. After each game, open the replay at two trades and note where you stood, how many enemy minions could reach you and your health after walking out. Compare the trades you took inside their wave with those you took outside it.

## Limits

Some abilities, effects and champions interact with minion targeting in unusual ways, and Riot can change targeting rules. Read current tooltips and patch notes when a result surprises you. The animation is a schematic model, not measured damage.
