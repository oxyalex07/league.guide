# Matchup preparation

- Stable lesson ID: MI28
- Slug: matchup-preparation
- Category: Micro
- Roles and level: All laners and supports; intermediate to advanced.
- Summary: Plan the first waves, key cooldowns and power windows before the game starts.
- Prerequisites: Trading, Power spikes, Cooldown tracking.
- Observable action: During loading, name the enemy's strongest early window and the cooldown you will play around.

## Short answer

High-ranked players rarely enter a lane without a plan. In the loading screen they already know when the opponent is strongest, which ability decides most trades and how the first few waves should go. You do not need a perfect matchup chart. You need three answers before the minions arrive: when am I stronger, when are they stronger, and what will I watch for to know which is true right now?

## How it works

Use the loading screen and the walk to lane to build a short plan.

1. **Early levels.** Who is stronger at levels one to three? Whoever reaches a key level first in a close matchup can often force a fight. Decide whether you want to push the first waves or play safely.
2. **The key cooldown.** Name the enemy ability that wins or loses most trades, such as a dash, a crowd-control spell or a shield. Trade when it is down; respect it when it is up.
3. **Power windows.** Note when each champion spikes, for example at their ultimate level or after a first major item. Plan to fight in your windows and avoid theirs.
4. **Jungle context.** Consider where both junglers are likely to start and which side of the map they will favour early.

## Hypothetical example

You are on a champion that is strong early, facing one that scales well but is weak before its first item. In loading, you decide to push the first waves, look for trades when the enemy uses their wave-clear ability, and keep the lane in front of you so they cannot farm safely. You also note that once they finish their first item, short trades will no longer favour you. When that item appears, you change to safer farming and look to move with your jungler instead.

## Going deeper

**Learn matchups through the key ability first.** You cannot memorise every champion's numbers, but you can learn the one or two abilities that matter most in each lane. Ask what the opponent must hit or use to win a fight, and how you avoid or punish it.

**Practice tool before ranked.** For a new matchup or champion, spend a few minutes in practice mode testing your combo, your trade patterns and how your abilities interact with minions. Ten focused minutes can save several bad games.

**Level advantages are real.** In many matchups, the first player to reach a key level gets a short window where they have an extra ability or more points. Pushing the first waves to get there first, or backing off when the opponent will get there first, is a common high-elo pattern.

**Rune and starting choices.** Choose your setup for the matchup, not only your champion. If you face heavy poke, consider options that help you sustain; if the lane is a race to scale, choose what helps you farm safely. Check current recommendations from reliable, up-to-date sources for your patch.

**Revisit the plan.** A plan made in loading is a starting point. If your opponent plays differently than expected, adjust after the first exchanges.

## Common mistake and correction

**Mistake:** Treating every lane the same and discovering the enemy's power only after losing a fight to it.

**Correction:** Make the three-question plan during loading. Write the enemy's key ability on a note if it helps.

## Practice

For five games, write one line during loading: their strongest window, the cooldown you will play around and your plan for the first waves. After the game, check whether the plan held and what you would change.

## Limits

Matchups change with balance patches, items and runes. Use up-to-date sources for numbers and builds. This lesson teaches a preparation habit, not a matchup database.
