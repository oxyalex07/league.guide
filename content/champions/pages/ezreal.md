# Ezreal

Protected sustained damage

## Playstyle

Use Mystic Shot to maintain pressure while moving between safe firing angles. Weave attacks when the threat range allows it.

## Strengths

Ranged spell pressure and a repositioning tool support flexible spacing.

## Weaknesses and how to respond

Missed shots and blocked lines reduce damage. Reposition around the frontline instead of abandoning all basic attacks at extreme range.

## Before the teamfight

Identify the nearest safe attack position and the enemy who can reach it. Keep an ally or controlled route between you and that threat.

## Your champion-specific teamfight plan

Keep Arcane Shift for the first dangerous engage unless a forward move is clearly safe. Fire through open lines rather than chasing a carry behind several blockers.

## During the fight

Attack the nearest target you can damage without entering lethal range. Move between attacks or casts, and shift forward only after the relevant enemy access is spent.

## When behind or unable to reach your target

Stay in the protected formation and accept a lower damage window. Clearing a safe wave or damaging a controlled frontline target is better than walking past allies to reach a carry.

## After the fight

Keep distance from surviving threats before helping with an objective. Recheck enemy positions instead of allowing a takedown to turn into an uncontrolled chase.

## Teamfight positioning

Damage from behind allied protection. The arrow is a firing line to a reachable enemy, not a movement route through your frontline.

As Ezreal, apply this rule before acting: Keep Arcane Shift for the first dangerous engage unless a forward move is clearly safe. Fire through open lines rather than chasing a carry behind several blockers. If the missing enemy could interrupt that plan, keep your current safe position and wait for information or allied setup.

## Early game: protect repeatable attacks

Use the early exchanges to learn which enemy action prevents you from attacking safely. It may be direct control, a flank route or pressure from a second opponent. Take attacks when that response is unavailable or when your partner can cover it, then move before the window closes. If you are farming a lane, do not trade away all your health merely to contest one minion. If your champion is used elsewhere, apply the same rule to the resource you are collecting. The goal is a repeatable pattern of damage and income, not a single impressive exchange that leaves you unable to stay on the map.

## Midgame: move with a reason and protection

Before moving toward a fight, identify the route that gives you a safe first attack position. Arriving early through an unchecked path can be worse than arriving slightly later with support. Catch available resources when the team does not need immediate presence, but account for how long it takes to return to the next contest. Avoid following a distant enemy while allies are resetting. If the team cannot protect a forward position, take a safer wave or wait in controlled space. Continued development matters because your role usually needs several attack opportunities, and a preventable death removes all of them for a time.

## Worked decision: when the first plan changes

Hypothetical practice situation. Your shots repeatedly hit the frontline while the carry stays behind it. Keep attacking a safe target and move to improve the firing line. Do not spend Arcane Shift forward just to create one clear shot. Save it for the engage that changes your position; consistent mixed attack and spell damage can outperform an unsafe carry chase.

## Read the enemy access tools

Separate the enemies you want to damage from the enemies who can stop you from dealing damage. The most urgent threat may be a durable diver, not the opposing carry. Against long-range pressure, move between casts and avoid standing in the same line as every teammate. Against flank access, hold a route back to an ally and check the edge before focusing on the frontline. Against a protected formation, accept safe damage on the nearest target until the formation changes. Your target can change during the fight; the rule is to choose the best damage available from a position that survives the relevant response.

## Manage the first attack window

Start from the safe edge and test whether the enemy is actually committing. Attack or cast, move with the formation, then check the next threat before repeating. Do not spend a defensive movement tool just because it adds a small amount of damage. If your kit has a special firing window, prepare the position before activating it so travel does not consume the benefit. If an ally moves away, your safe area may shrink immediately even when no enemy has moved. Stop attacking briefly when needed to rebuild the position. That pause can preserve many more attacks than standing still through the next engage.

## Adapt when ahead, behind or under pressure

When ahead, keep dealing damage through protection rather than proving the lead with an unsupported duel. Enemies may need to commit more tools to reach you, which makes patience even more valuable. When behind, reduce unnecessary exposure and contribute to a controlled target; a lower damage window is still better than no window. When your protection disappears, move toward the nearest controlled route and accept that the fight may have ended for you. Do not cross the enemy line to reach a teammate who cannot actually help. Recovery begins by keeping the next useful farming and fighting opportunity available.

## After a takedown: advance one safe step

A falling opponent changes the formation but does not reveal every remaining threat. Check the survivor with the best access before moving forward to clean up. If a reset or movement reward is part of your kit, use it to reach another safe position rather than assuming it grants immunity. Help the team convert the fight through a reachable objective or wave, then reset when health and resources no longer support contact. Review the first forward step after each takedown. That is often the moment where a strong attack pattern turns into a preventable death and gives away the objective the team had just earned.

## Build a personal matchup plan

Before the first meaningful exchange, identify three things about the opponent: how they begin contact, what makes their damage dangerous and how they leave. Compare those actions with your own kit plan instead of labeling the matchup simply good or bad. Pick one observable opening, such as an important response being used or an ally creating setup. Also name one condition that cancels the attempt, such as another enemy arriving or your escape route closing. Use the opening only while the conditions remain true. After a lost exchange, change one decision rather than assuming the entire matchup is impossible. This guide teaches that decision process; it does not provide a numerical matchup ranking or a fixed build for every patch.

## A focused practice session

Begin in practice mode by checking the current tooltips and rehearsing the key sequence without an opponent. Add movement before trying to perform it quickly. Next, practise the same sequence with a deliberate stop: miss the setup, change the target or move away before the continuation. This teaches the choice to cancel rather than only the choice to commit. In a normal game, focus on one measurable behavior for three relevant situations. Record whether you checked the threat and exit before acting. A win does not automatically mean the behavior was correct, and a death does not automatically mean the opening was wrong. Keep the practice target narrow enough that you can describe the next change in one sentence.

## Review without rewriting what you knew

Pause a replay just before your first committed move and use only the information visible to your team at that moment. List visible enemies, missing threats, nearby allies and the important resources you actually had. Predict the next action before continuing playback. Then compare the prediction with the response: was the error an inaccurate assumption, a missed mechanic or a decision to continue after conditions changed? Review one successful exchange as well as one failure so you can repeat the good setup. Do not use later knowledge of a hidden opponent to pretend their exact location was obvious. The useful improvement is a better check, safer route or clearer stopping condition for the next similar situation.

What would you do if your intended target moved out of safe reach? Where should you stand when the first sequence ends? Compare your answers with the playstyle, weakness and worked decision sections. Repeat the exercise after practice and explain which decision you changed and why.
